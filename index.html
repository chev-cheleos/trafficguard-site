What TrafficGuard is

TrafficGuard lives in the menu bar and shows how much network data each application on your Mac sends and receives — right now, today, and over the last 7 and 30 days, split by the network you were on. With TrafficGuard Pro it can also stop a chosen application from reaching the network. Everything it measures stays on your Mac; see the privacy policy.

Getting started

1. Open the panel. Click the shield in the menu bar. TrafficGuard has no Dock icon; the panel is the app.

2. Allow the network extension. The first time you enable measuring, macOS asks you to allow a system extension. If you missed the prompt, open System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions › Network Extensions and allow TrafficGuard. Its configuration then appears as “TrafficGuard Transparent Proxy” under System Settings › Network › VPN & Filters. Settings › Permissions in the app shows the state of each permission and opens the right pane.

3. Location is optional. macOS treats a Wi-Fi network’s name as location data, so TrafficGuard needs Location (When In Use) only to label your traffic by network and to switch network profiles. Decline it and everything else keeps working; networks are simply shown as “unknown”.

Free, trial and Pro

Monitoring is free, forever. Live per-application traffic, today’s totals and the desktop widgets never require a purchase.

TrafficGuard Pro is a one-time purchase — no subscription. It adds per-application blocking, the 7- and 30-day history leaderboards, network profiles and the data cap. Buy it from the Upgrade button in the panel or from Settings › License.

7-day free trial. Settings › License › Start 7-Day Free Trial unlocks every Pro feature for seven days through the App Store at no charge; nothing to cancel. When it ends, every application you blocked is released automatically and monitoring continues. One trial per Apple Account.

Bought Pro on another Mac, or reinstalled? Settings › License › Restore Purchases brings it back through the App Store.

Common questions

“Nothing is being measured.” Open Settings › Network Filter and check that the filter is on, then Settings › Permissions to confirm the network extension is allowed. Toggling the filter off and on re-registers it with macOS.

“Blocking has stopped.” The trial ended, or Pro could not be confirmed with the App Store. Your block list is kept; buy Pro, or Restore Purchases, and blocking resumes with the same rules.

“An application is called System or Unattributed.” macOS did not attribute that flow to an application reliably, so TrafficGuard shows the truth rather than a guess. Those rows can be measured but not blocked.

“I want today’s numbers reset.” Settings › Traffic & History › Reset Today’s Statistics. Daily history keeps 30 days and then deletes the oldest day by itself.

“The widgets are blank.” Open the panel once so TrafficGuard writes the figures the widgets read, and check that TrafficGuard is running — the widgets say “Waiting for TrafficGuard” when it is not.

“How do I remove it completely?” Settings › About › Remove Network Filter… removes the configuration, the system extension, all stored data and the app’s preferences; then drag TrafficGuard to the Trash. Dragging it to the Trash first leaves the extension registered with macOS, so use the button.
