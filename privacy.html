TrafficGuard Privacy Policy

Effective 9 September 2026  ·  TrafficGuard 1.0.0 (69)  ·  macOS

TrafficGuard measures how much network data each application on your Mac sends and receives. Everything it measures stays on your Mac.

There is no TrafficGuard server, no account, no analytics, and no advertising. Albatros Solutions never receives your traffic data, and neither does anybody else. The only network connection TrafficGuard itself causes is Apple’s own StoreKit talking to the App Store when you buy or restore TrafficGuard Pro, or start the free 7-day trial, which the App Store records as a purchase at no charge — and TrafficGuard never sees your payment details.

Who we are

TrafficGuard is developed and published by Albatros Solutions. You can reach us at albatrossolutions@icloud.com, which is also the address shown inside the app under Settings › About.

What TrafficGuard observes

When you enable its network filter, TrafficGuard runs a macOS transparent proxy system extension built on Apple’s Network Extension framework. macOS hands that extension information about each network flow on your Mac. From that, TrafficGuard keeps four things:

  • Which application opened the flow, resolved from the flow’s audit token to a code-signing identifier or bundle identifier. Flows macOS does not attribute reliably are shown honestly as System or Unattributed rather than guessed at.
  • How many bytes that application sent and received.
  • When it was last active, and how many of its flows are open.
  • Which network the bytes were used on — a Wi-Fi network name, or “wired”, or “unknown” when the name is unavailable.

That is the whole list. It is metadata about volume and ownership, not about content.

What TrafficGuard never sees or records

TrafficGuard does not read, store, or transmit:

  • packet contents or payloads of any kind
  • URLs, web page contents, or browsing history
  • domain names or DNS queries
  • messages, email, documents, or file contents
  • passwords, tokens, or credentials
  • your geographic location, or any coordinate

TrafficGuard does not decrypt anything it proxies, and makes no attempt to. The extension counts bytes and passes flows through untouched.

Why TrafficGuard asks for Location permission

This is the app’s most surprising permission, so here is the plain explanation.

TrafficGuard’s Network Profiles feature lets you apply different rules on different Wi-Fi networks — blocking backup and update traffic on a phone hotspot, say, but not at home. To do that, the app has to know the name of the Wi-Fi network you are on.

On macOS 14 and later, Apple classifies the Wi-Fi network name (SSID) as location data. The system call that returns it, CWWiFiClient.interface().ssid(), returns nothing unless the app holds Location (When In Use) authorization. That authorization is the only way to read a network name; there is no narrower permission to ask for.

So, precisely:

  • TrafficGuard requests Location solely to read the Wi-Fi network name.
  • TrafficGuard never calls startUpdatingLocation() and never reads a latitude, a longitude, an altitude, or any other coordinate. No location data of any kind is stored or transmitted.
  • If you decline, TrafficGuard keeps working. Network-name matching switches off and you choose the profile you want by hand. Nothing else is lost.

The Wi-Fi network names you have used are kept on your Mac, alongside your traffic history, so the app can label that history by network and match your profiles.

What is stored, and where

Everything TrafficGuard records is written to your own Mac, in two places only TrafficGuard and its extension can read.

App Group container

~/Library/Group Containers/group.com.albatrossolutions.TrafficGuard/

trial-v1.jsonwhen your free 7-day trial started — a local copy of the App Store’s own record
widget-v1.jsonthe figures your desktop widgets display

The app’s own preferences

Stored in UserDefaults and readable only by TrafficGuard: your network profiles and the Wi-Fi network names bound to them, your data cap, your display settings, the state of the Guard switch, and whether the app has shown you its panel on first launch.

Your keychain

One item, readable only by TrafficGuard: the date on which you started the free 7-day trial, and nothing else. It is kept there rather than in the container so that Remove Network Filter… — which deletes every file above — cannot hand back a trial that has already been used; it therefore survives that removal and survives deleting the app. Because the trial is a free in-app purchase, the App Store also holds the record that you took it, in your Apple Account, as it does for any purchase; the keychain item is the copy this Mac can read without asking Apple.

Launch at Login

If you switch it on in Settings › General, it is a login item registered with macOS itself. TrafficGuard stores nothing for it; macOS does, and System Settings › General › Login Items & Extensions shows and controls it. It is off until you turn it on.

None of this is synced, uploaded, or shared with anyone. It is subject only to whatever ordinary macOS backup you have configured yourself — Time Machine, say — which is under your control, not ours.

Diagnostic log lines

TrafficGuard writes occasional diagnostic notices to macOS’s own unified log, the log you can read in Console.app. Since build 66, some of those lines name an application in order to explain how a flow was attributed — for example, “Attributed /usr/libexec/foo to parent app com.example.bar”. Each distinct message is written only once per run, so the log is not flooded.

Those lines live in the macOS system log on your Mac. TrafficGuard does not collect them, does not upload them, and has no mechanism to send them anywhere. They leave your Mac only if you yourself choose to share a system diagnostic report with Apple or with us.

What leaves your Mac

Traffic data Never leaves No server, no upload, no third party.
Purchases StoreKit → Apple Apple handles payment; we never see it.

Traffic data: nothing. There is no TrafficGuard server. The app contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising SDK, and no third-party library of any kind.

That is verifiable rather than merely asserted. Across the three shipped 1.0.0 (69) binaries — the app, the widget extension, and the network filter extension — there are zero URLSession or other HTTP-client symbols, zero linked libraries outside Apple’s own system frameworks, and no URL belonging to Albatros Solutions or to any third party. Every URL present anywhere in the bundle is an Apple certificate-authority, OCSP, or CRL endpoint placed there by code signing, and is used by macOS rather than by TrafficGuard.

Purchases: StoreKit, to Apple. TrafficGuard Pro is a non-consumable in-app purchase sold through Apple’s StoreKit 2. When you buy it, restore it, or when the app checks your entitlement, StoreKit connects to Apple over the system’s own secure connection. TrafficGuard receives back an Apple-signed transaction result and stores only whether Pro is active. Your card number, billing address, and Apple Account details go to Apple, and are never seen by TrafficGuard or by Albatros Solutions. Apple’s handling of them is governed by Apple’s Privacy Policy.

The free 7-day trial works the same way, at no charge. Starting it is an in-app purchase at a price of zero — the form Apple prescribes for a trial — which the App Store records against your Apple Account so that it can be taken once and restored like any purchase. TrafficGuard keeps only the date it started. No card is charged, and nothing beyond that StoreKit exchange leaves your Mac.

Tracking: none. TrafficGuard does not track you across apps or websites, declares no tracking domains, and does not use the App Tracking Transparency framework, because it has nothing to ask you for.

How long data is kept

  • Today’s traffic totals roll over at local midnight.
  • Daily history is capped at 30 days. When a thirty-first day is recorded, the oldest day is deleted from the file. There is no longer-term archive.
  • Rules, profiles, and settings are kept until you change or delete them.

How to delete everything

In the app

Settings › About › Remove Network Filter…

Confirming that removes, in order: the network configuration, which also clears the “TrafficGuard Transparent Proxy” entry from System Settings; the system extension itself; every file in the App Group container listed above; TrafficGuard’s entire preferences domain; and the Launch at Login item, if you had switched it on. Nothing of TrafficGuard’s is left running or stored, except the single keychain record that your free trial was used — the dialog says so, and it is kept precisely so that removal cannot hand a spent trial back. macOS may ask you to restart to finish retiring the extension, after which you can drag TrafficGuard to the Trash.

Two narrower options are also available:

  • Settings › Traffic & History › Traffic Data › Reset Today’s Statistics clears today’s totals only.
  • Settings › General › Blocking Recovery › Allow All Applications clears every block rule.

One caution: dragging TrafficGuard to the Trash without removing the filter first leaves the system extension installed and running, because macOS registers that extension with the system rather than with the app bundle. Remove the filter first.

Because we hold no data about you off your Mac, there is nothing for us to delete on request, and no account to close. Deleting it on your Mac deletes all of it.

Children’s privacy

TrafficGuard is a general-purpose utility for macOS. It is not directed at children, it collects no personal information from anyone of any age, and it has no accounts, no profiles, no messaging, and no user-generated content. Because nothing is transmitted or collected, no information from a child under 13 — or the equivalent age in your jurisdiction — is ever received by Albatros Solutions.

Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR and the CCPA give you rights to access, correct, export, and delete the personal data a company holds about you. Albatros Solutions holds none: there is no database, no server, and no record of you. Your data lives only on your own Mac, where you already have full access to it and can delete it at any time using the steps above.

We do not sell or share personal information, because we do not have any.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of TrafficGuard changes what it stores, or introduces any network communication of its own, this policy will be updated before that version is released and the effective date at the top will change. Material changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes.
